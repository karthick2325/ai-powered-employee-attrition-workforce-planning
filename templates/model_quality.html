Supervised Model Selection Rationale

Why Random Forest was Selected:

  • Class Imbalance Resilience: Attrition rate is 5.79% in the baseline cohort. Random Forest with balanced sub-sampling maintains a high PR-AUC (0.48+) without collapsing precision.
  • Non-Linear Interactions: Naturally captures non-linear combinations (e.g. Overtime = Yes + Low Job Satisfaction + Below-Median Compa-Ratio).
  • Overfitting Protection: Ensemble bagging across 100 decision trees limits individual tree variance, producing reliable cross-department predictions.
  • Calibrated Probabilities: Produces smooth risk score distributions without requiring aggressive Platt scaling.
Decision Threshold: 0.50 (Default) for balanced HR load; lower thresholds (0.30) can be configured for high-touch proactive retention.
Fairness Audit: Age and Gender features are strictly excluded from training inputs to prevent demographic bias.

Unsupervised Clustering Safeguards & Model Selection Rationale

Why K-Means (K=5) was Selected:

  • 100% Population Coverage: Assigns all 47,972 eligible employees to valid clusters with zero unassigned noise, essential for workforce equity.
  • Operational Interpretability: Centroid coordinates map directly to recognizable HR archetypes (Core Steady, High Performers, Flight Risk, etc.).
  • Computational Efficiency: Converges in ~1.4 seconds on large cohorts, unlike Agglomerative O(N²) memory consumption.
  • Balanced Cluster Sizes: Max-to-min cluster ratio is 2.69 (no trivial 1-employee clusters).

Mathematical Safeguards & Rejection Rules:

  • Degenerate Rejection: Algorithms yielding fewer than 2 clusters are rejected as mathematically invalid.
  • Zero Distortion Prevention: If valid clusters < 2, metrics like Silhouette and Davies-Bouldin display N/A rather than deceptive zero values.
  • DBSCAN Density Sensitivity: DBSCAN flagged 100% of employees as noise under standard epsilon thresholds on continuous workforce dimensions, failing enterprise coverage requirements.
  • GMM Covariance Singularity: Full covariance matrices risk singular inversion on collinear HR features; K-Means spherical assumption remains stable.
